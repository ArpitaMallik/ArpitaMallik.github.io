I am Arpita Mallik, a recent Computer Science and Engineering graduate from CUET with a research focus on artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and large language models.

I study why AI models behave the way they do: why they get some answers right, why they fail on others, and why they sometimes give up an answer they already know. I want to use that understanding to build models people can trust, especially in healthcare and in languages like Bangla that most models are not built for. You can find my ongoing work in my CV.

Research Interests

I want to understand what happens inside a model when it succeeds or fails, and turn that into better ways to test and fix it.

  • Model Behavior & Interpretability What models represent inside, and how that makes them right or wrong.
  • Trustworthy AI Why models give up a correct answer under pressure, and how to make them more reliable.
  • Meaningful Evaluation Whether our metrics measure real correctness or only surface quality.
  • High Stakes Domains & Low Resource Languages Healthcare, law, and Bangla, where these failures matter most and where most of my work is.

Undergraduate Thesis

2025 – 2026

When High Scores Mislead: Evaluating Trust in Bangla Women’s Health Question Answering

B.Sc. thesis, CUET · Supervised by Sabiha Anan

Built a 3,002 pair Bangla and Banglish women’s health QA dataset and fine tuned seven models on it. Found that BERTScore only weakly separates right answers from wrong ones: 12% of answers in the top quarter of scores were judged incorrect. The LLM judge was checked against a medical doctor on 105 answers (Cohen’s κ = 0.61 for correctness).
